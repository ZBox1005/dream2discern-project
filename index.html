Overview

Dream2Discern at a glance

  1. 01

    Corpus augmentation is costly and untargeted

    Runtime auditors learn from a limited labeled corpus. New trajectories need rollouts and verification, edits can invalidate outcome labels, and broader coverage does not reveal hard cases.

  2. 02

    Dream constructs hard latent variants

    With the auditor fixed, a bounded, low-rank perturbation of its query and value projections is chosen to increase batch prediction loss, yielding hard latent variants of recorded prefixes.

  3. 03

    Discern learns from them with the original labels

    The auditor learns from these variants with the recorded labels as they are regenerated each batch. At runtime the perturbation is discarded and a calibrated threshold predicts failure.

Abstract

Reliable deployment of LLM-based multi-agent systems calls for runtime auditors that predict terminal outcomes from observed execution prefixes. These auditors typically learn from a limited corpus of trajectories with verified outcomes. Expanding this corpus requires costly execution and verification, yet broader failure coverage alone provides no explicit criterion for identifying prefixes that the current auditor finds difficult to classify. To this end, we propose Dream2Discern, a runtime auditor trained through implicit augmentation guided by its own prediction loss. During Dream, we select perturbations of the auditor's representation operators to increase prediction loss on recorded prefixes, yielding hard latent variants. During Discern, the auditor learns to predict execution outcomes from these variants using the original labels. The variants are regenerated as the auditor learns, turning a limited corpus into a renewable source of hard examples and requiring no additional trajectory collection or outcome verification. Extensive evaluations on AFTraj-2K and StepShield show that Dream2Discern outperforms competitive failure auditors and achieves over 90% trajectory-level weighted accuracy on both benchmarks while balancing prediction accuracy and timeliness.

Research question

Can we identify and exploit the current auditor's hard cases without acquiring additional labeled trajectories?

02 · Key insight

Prediction loss as the criterion for hard variants

Our key insight is to use the auditor's own prediction loss as a criterion for deciding which variants to construct.

Preliminary evidence

Edits do not improve held-out prediction, while a loss-guided perturbation makes it harder

Original and edited prefixes, encoded by the same frozen LLM, overlap in a shared PCA projection. With the encoder and training budget fixed, adding verified edits does not lower held-out class-balanced BCE. A perturbation selected only on training prefixes, applied temporarily with the original head fixed, raises the same held-out loss.

Step 1 · Labeled prefixes

A class-balanced batch of recorded prefixes

We form a class-balanced batch from recorded success and failure prefixes, keeping their inputs and outcome labels unchanged throughout the training steps.

Step 2 · Dream

Construct hard latent variants

With the auditor fixed, we search for shared, bounded, low-rank perturbations to its query and value projection weights that increase batch prediction loss.

Ascent → Rank-r SVD → Accept

Take the batch-loss gradient at V = 0 for each adapted query and value projection, in scaled coordinates Cj = ωjGj.

Normalize the scaled gradient and keep its leading r singular components per projection, giving a candidate within radius γ.

Halve the candidate until the measured batch loss strictly exceeds the loss at V = 0. At most six points are tested; if none is accepted, V = 0.

Learn from them with the original labels

Holding the selected perturbation fixed, we update the LoRA adapters and prediction head to minimize the loss on the resulting hard latent variants.

Score each prefix, perturbation discarded

We discard the perturbation and apply a calibrated threshold to each prefix score to predict failure during execution.

04 · Results

Over 90% trajectory-level weighted accuracy on both benchmarks

Benchmarks

AFTraj-2K: multi-agent executions in math, coding and tool use, tested in-domain. StepShield: safety-monitoring trajectories, tested on unseen incident templates.

Metrics

AUROC and FPR95 from each trajectory's maximum prefix score; wACC, trajectory-level weighted accuracy; TWA, time-weighted accuracy rewarding earlier detection.

Shown models

Open-weight judges: Qwen3-8B, LlamaGuard3-8B and Qwen3-32B; closed-source judges remain. SAFE, H&S and TRACES use Qwen3-8B; Dream2Discern shows 4B and 8B.

On in-domain AFTraj-2K, Dream2Discern leads all four metrics. With Qwen3-8B, it outperforms SAFE, Hide-and-Seek and TRACES on every metric, averaged over three seeds.

On out-of-domain StepShield, Dream2Discern leads wACC and TWA. LLM judges are more competitive here, yet the 4B auditor exceeds Claude-Sonnet-4.6 in wACC and TWA, and its wACC advantage over failure auditors is substantially larger than on AFTraj-2K.

Detection accuracy–timeliness trade-off

With Qwen3-8B, we vary the nominal false-alarm level α from 0.01 to 1.00 and plot wACC against mean normalized detection time over failed trajectories, counting undetected failures as one.

On AFTraj-2K, Dream2Discern achieves higher wACC when early detection is prioritized.

On StepShield, the gains extend across a broad range of operating points.
